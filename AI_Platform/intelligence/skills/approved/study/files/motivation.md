# Not Starting, Not Continuing, and Getting Back

A plan that is not executed is not a planning problem. Almost every "I have no discipline" is a task that is too vague to start, an outcome that feels already lost, or a load that has been unsustainable for weeks.

**Contents:** [Diagnose Before Fixing](#diagnose-before-fixing) · [Starting](#starting) · [Continuing](#continuing) · [Distraction and the Phone](#distraction-and-the-phone) · [After a Bad Mark](#after-a-bad-mark) · [Coming Back After a Gap](#coming-back-after-a-gap) · [Burnout](#burnout) · [Red Flags](#red-flags) · [What Not to Do](#what-not-to-do)

**Before treating a motivation problem as a motivation problem**, read `session-log/<year>-<month>.md` and `## Due`. Weeks of logged blocks with a collapsing review queue is a load problem; no logged blocks at all is a starting problem. They have opposite fixes.

## Diagnose Before Fixing

| What is happening | Actual cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sits down, does something else | Task too vague to begin | Shrink the entry to a named first action (below) |
| Avoids one specific course entirely | Expects to fail at it; avoidance is protective | Start with its easiest recoverable win, and make the first block absurdly short |
| Starts fine, drifts after 15 minutes | Block too long, or the task has no visible outcome | Shorten the block; name the exit artifact (`sessions.md`) |
| Studies constantly, nothing sticks | Not a motivation problem at all | Retrieval, not hours (SKILL.md Rule 1) |
| Plan abandoned in week 3, every term | Plan was built at 100% of the budget | Rebuild at 80% with catch-up blocks (`planning.md`) |
| Everything feels pointless, including things previously enjoyed | Possible burnout or something clinical | Red Flags, below |
| Cannot start *anything*, for weeks, across life | Outside this skill's scope | Red Flags, below |

Fixing a load problem with motivation techniques makes it worse, and fixing a starting problem with a better plan is the most common wasted afternoon in the domain.

## Starting

- **The entry action must be nameable in five words and take under two minutes.** "Study chapter 3" fails; "answer yesterday's three missed questions" starts (`sessions.md`).
- **Reduce the block until it is not worth avoiding.** Ten minutes, honestly ten. The resistance is to the imagined three hours, and it disappears once started — the point of the small block is that it is started, not that it is small.
- **Same time, same place, same first action.** Making the decision once removes the decision that gets lost every day.
- **Start before feeling ready.** Motivation follows action far more reliably than it precedes it; waiting to feel like studying is waiting on the wrong variable.
- **Remove the friction physically**: materials out and open the night before, tabs closed, phone in another room. Every step between sitting and working is a place to stop.
- **When avoidance is about a specific fear** — the material is genuinely not understood — name it and make the first block a diagnostic, not a performance: five questions, unscored, to find out exactly where the floor is.

## Continuing

- **Track blocks done, not hours felt.** `session-log/<year>-<month>.md` is the honest record and it is also the streak.
- **A streak's value is the not-breaking**, so define the minimum day: one review pass. A day with the minimum done is not a broken streak, which is what stops one bad day becoming a bad fortnight.
- **Never miss twice.** One missed session is noise; two consecutive is the start of the pattern that ends terms.
- **Make progress visible where it is real**: topics moved to `exam-ready` in `## Topics`, misses closed in `errors.md`. Hours logged are not progress and tracking them rewards the wrong thing.
- **Schedule rest deliberately**, including one full day. Unscheduled rest is taken as guilt-laden drift and restores nothing.
- **Reward on completion of the block, not the outcome.** Outcomes are weeks away; the behaviour needing reinforcement is sitting down.
- The strongest external structure is another person expecting you (`groups.md`).

## Distraction and the Phone

- **Out of the room beats face-down beats notifications off.** Presence within reach is its own condition, independent of whether it buzzes.
- **A blocker helps only while the friction to disable it is real.** One with a password held by someone else works; one uninstalled in ten seconds does not.
- **Single-tab discipline** for the study material, and a paper list for anything that needs looking up later. The lookup that opens a browser mid-block ends the block.
- **Notice the trigger, not the app**: distraction almost always begins at a point of difficulty. The urge to check something is a reliable signal that you just hit the part worth studying.
- **Boredom is the point of contact with the material.** Sitting with it for two more minutes is the trained skill (`sessions.md`, stuck ladder).

## After a Bad Mark

- **Wait a day before analysing.** Same-day analysis is emotion, not diagnosis, and the conclusions drawn are usually wrong and permanent-sounding.
- **Get the actual paper and the mark scheme.** A mark without the script tells you nothing about cause.
- **Classify every lost mark** by cause (SKILL.md Rule 6). The distribution decides the response: mostly `out of time` is a pacing problem, mostly `never encoded` is a coverage problem, mostly `misread` is a question-parsing problem. These have nothing in common and studying harder addresses none of them.
- **Recompute what is still possible** honestly: `needed = (target − earned) ÷ remaining_weight`. If the target is gone, say so and re-aim — planning around impossible arithmetic is how a bad mark becomes a failed course.
- **Check the practical options with the institution**: resit rules, capped marks, mitigating circumstances, whether the module can be repeated. These are policy questions with deadlines.
- **Write the post-mortem as an artifact** (`memory-template.md`). A failure that produced a written diagnosis is the cheapest tuition available; one that produced only a bad week repeats.
- One bad mark predicts very little on its own. Two with the same cause is the pattern worth acting on.

## Coming Back After a Gap

- **Do not attempt to catch up on everything.** The plan is rebuilt from today's date with today's remaining weeks (`planning.md`); the missed work is scope to cut, not debt to repay.
- **Restart with the review queue at reduced size**, not with new material. It is the only part whose collapse compounds (`spacing.md`).
- **The first session back is deliberately easy and short.** Its only job is to happen.
- **Expect the first week back to feel worse than the gap did.** Re-entry is uncomfortable and temporary; it is not evidence that the return was a mistake.
- Where the gap had a cause that has not gone away — illness, work, a crisis at home — the rebuilt plan is built for that reality, not for the student who existed in September.

## Burnout

Distinguishable from laziness by three signs together: it follows a sustained period of high effort, effort no longer produces results, and the loss of interest generalizes beyond studying.

- **The fix is load, not willpower.** Reduce scope on purpose and say what was cut; a smaller plan actually executed outperforms a large one abandoned.
- **Protect sleep first** (SKILL.md Rule 9), then one full rest day, then reintroduce blocks at half length.
- **Remove one commitment for real** rather than optimizing all of them. Optimizing is what produced the state.
- **Cynicism about the course is a symptom**, not a conclusion to act on while in the state. Postpone irreversible decisions — dropping out, switching programmes — until after a genuine recovery period.
- Recurrent burnout across terms means the standing load is wrong, and that is a planning conversation with real numbers (`planning.md`).

## Red Flags

Anything in this table suspends the study protocols. These are not motivation problems and no technique in this skill addresses them.

| Signal (observable) | Suspicion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sleeping under ~5 hours for a week or more, or unable to sleep despite exhaustion | Sustained deprivation or a sleep disorder; retention and judgement are already impaired | Stop the evening blocks; sleep is now the intervention. Persisting past a week → clinician (`sleep`) |
| Panic symptoms in exams or at the thought of studying — chest tightness, unable to breathe, unable to enter the room | Anxiety beyond exam nerves | Institution's counselling service or a clinician; do not treat with pacing tactics |
| Loss of interest, hopelessness, or inability to function that persists beyond two weeks and spans more than studying | Possible depression | Route to a clinician or the institution's health service; the study plan waits |
| Stimulant use to study — prescription drugs not prescribed to them, escalating caffeine, anything to stay awake | Substance risk, and a load problem underneath | Stop; address the load, and involve a clinician where use is established |
| Not eating, or eating only at exam time; rapid unintended weight change | Physical health at risk | Clinician; study planning is not the live issue |
| Any expression of self-harm or of not wanting to be here | Emergency | Stop everything and direct to emergency services or a crisis line in their country, and to someone they trust, now |

Escalation is not a failure of the plan; continuing the plan through any of these is.

## What Not to Do

| Approach | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Guilt and self-criticism as motivation | Increases avoidance of the specific task; the aversion attaches to the course |
| Restarting with a bigger, more detailed plan | The previous plan was not too small; it was not started (`planning.md`) |
| Studying to exhaustion to "make up" a bad week | Buys hours from the mechanism that stores learning (Rule 9) |
| Comparing to classmates' reported hours | Self-reported study time is unreliable in both directions, and hours are not the variable anyway |
| Waiting for motivation | Action produces it; the wait is the failure mode itself |
| Guaranteeing a specific grade to feel better | Sets up a second failure; plan against the arithmetic, not the hope |

**When a motivation intervention is tried**, record it with its outcome in `## What Works`, and any recurring pattern — always avoids this course, always collapses in week 3 — in `## Pain Points` with dates. A post-mortem after a bad mark or a burnout period is an artifact with its own `## Boxes` line: the same collapse repeats every term until someone writes down what preceded it (`memory-template.md`).
