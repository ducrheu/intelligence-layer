# Partners, Groups, Tutors, and Office Hours

Other people are the cheapest source of two things a solo student cannot generate: questions they would not have asked themselves, and an expectation that makes the session happen.

**Contents:** [When a Group Beats Studying Alone](#when-a-group-beats-studying-alone) · [Running a Group That Works](#running-a-group-that-works) · [Study Partners](#study-partners) · [Teaching as Retrieval](#teaching-as-retrieval) · [Office Hours](#office-hours) · [Tutors](#tutors) · [Online Communities](#online-communities) · [Accountability Without a Group](#accountability-without-a-group)

**Before a group or tutor session**, read `errors.md` for the open loops and `## Topics` for what is at criterion. A session spent on what everyone already knows is the default outcome, and the error log is what prevents it.

## When a Group Beats Studying Alone

| Task | Alone or group |
|---|---|
| First encounter with new material | Alone — group reading is slow and passive |
| Retrieval practice and quizzing | Group, clearly: someone else's question is harder than your own |
| Explaining a concept to check understanding | Group — the listener finds the gap you would have skipped |
| Problem sets after an individual attempt | Group, for comparing approaches; before the attempt, it is a demonstration |
| Essay planning and argument stress-testing | Group — the counter-argument you cannot see is the marks you are missing |
| Memorization drilling | Either; group adds pace and consequence |
| Anything under deadline pressure | Alone, unless the deliverable is genuinely shared |

The rule: group time is for **output** — quizzing, explaining, defending. Input is done alone beforehand.

## Running a Group That Works

Most study groups are social hours with textbooks present. Four rules change that:

1. **Everyone arrives with prepared questions** — five each, written, from their own error log. No preparation, no session; this is the rule that determines whether the group survives.
2. **A fixed agenda and a fixed end time.** Topics in order, minutes each. Open-ended sessions become social within twenty minutes and nobody can say when it happened.
3. **Rotate the explainer.** Whoever explains a topic gets the retrieval; the rotation is what stops the strongest member doing all the learning while everyone else watches.
4. **Three to four people.** Two is a partnership with different dynamics; five or more guarantees passengers and side conversations.

Formats worth using: each person teaches one topic for ten minutes and takes questions; whole-group free recall of a topic onto a shared board, then diff against the source; a timed past paper sat simultaneously and then marked against each other's reasoning.

**Cut the group** if two sessions in a row produce nothing that shows up in `## Topics`. A group that is enjoyable and unproductive is expensive precisely because it feels like studying.

## Study Partners

- **One reliable partner outperforms a group** for accountability: sessions are easier to schedule and a missed one is visible.
- **Match on reliability, not on ability.** A stronger partner helps, a weaker partner helps (explaining is retrieval), and an unreliable partner costs the slot.
- **Parallel work with a shared start and end** — same room or a call, silent throughout, five minutes at the end to report what each covered — is the lightest useful structure and works even across different courses.
- **Quiz each other from prepared questions**, not from the textbook opened at random.
- Record who they are and what they are good for in the shared `contacts.md`, with the course as context (`memory-template.md`).

## Teaching as Retrieval

Explaining to someone who can ask questions is one of the highest-value study activities available, and it works even when the listener knows nothing.

- **Teach from memory, materials closed.** Teaching with the notes open is reading aloud.
- **The listener's job is to interrupt** at every step that does not follow. A silent audience gives no signal.
- **The point where the explanation stalls is the gap** — that goes to `errors.md` with cause `not retrievable`, and it is the next thing studied.
- **Explaining to a non-specialist forces the compression** that reveals whether the structure is understood or memorized (the Feynman procedure, `subjects.md`).
- Without a person available, explaining to a recorder works: the act of producing continuous speech is most of the effect, and the playback finds the vague parts.

## Office Hours

The most under-used resource in higher education, and the person there usually writes the exam.

- **Bring the attempt and the specific blockage**, never "I don't understand chapter 4". "I get to this line and I do not know why the sign flips" gets a two-minute answer; the vague version gets a re-lecture.
- **Ask about emphasis, not answers**: what a good answer to this kind of question looks like, which parts of the syllabus matter most, whether the format has changed. These are legitimate and rarely asked.
- **Go early in the term.** The queue in week 11 is long and the answers are rushed.
- **Write down the answer immediately** and convert it into a question for the bank that evening (`notes.md`).
- Asking a question early establishes that you are engaged, which affects the marginal decisions on borderline marks in ways nobody documents.
- The lecturer or TA goes in the shared `contacts.md` with their office-hour slot as context — the detail that is always looked up and never remembered.

## Tutors

- **Hire for a diagnosed gap with an end condition**, not as ongoing insurance. "Get me from unable to solve these to solving them unaided by the exam" is a brief; "help with maths" is a subscription.
- **A tutor who solves problems while you watch is not tutoring.** The session should be mostly you working with them observing and interrupting.
- **Bring the error log.** A tutor given the actual failure pattern works on the real gap in the first session rather than diagnosing for three.
- **Ask for the method, then work an instance unaided in front of them.** That is the only proof the session transferred.
- Peer tutors and postgraduate teaching assistants are often cheaper and closer to the exam than professional tutors, and they usually sat the same paper.
- Record the tutor in `contacts.md` and the arrangement's end condition in `## Courses`; sessions and their outcomes go in the session log like any other block.

## Online Communities

- **Course forums and subject communities are excellent for specific questions** and terrible for study planning: the loudest advice is generally the most extreme.
- **Post the attempt, not the question.** A question with visible work gets a useful answer; a bare question gets links.
- **Check any claim about the exam against the syllabus and past papers.** Rumours about what "always comes up" propagate every year and are often wrong.
- **Shared notes and decks are unverified** until you check them against your own syllabus (`flashcards.md`).
- Sharing coursework, even after submission, is an integrity risk in many institutions (`coursework.md`).
- Time in the community counts as study only when it produces retrievals — which is almost never.

## Accountability Without a Group

- **Tell one person the specific commitment and the check-in time.** Specific and dated beats an intention announced to nobody.
- **A public log** — a shared sheet of blocks completed — provides the same expectation with less coordination.
- **Booking a slot with a real other person** (a tutor, a partner, a library room) makes the session external and much harder to skip.
- Where none of this is available, `## Due` and the session log are the substitute: an overdue row stated out loud at the start of a session is a weak accountability signal, and weak beats none (`motivation.md`).

**After any group, tutor or office-hours session**, write the gaps it exposed to `errors.md` with their causes, log the block in `session-log/<year>-<month>.md`, and update `## Topics` for anything that moved. People go to the shared `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` — one row per person, identified by their key, updated in place — and a verdict on whether the group or tutor is working goes to `## What Works` with the date (`memory-template.md`).
