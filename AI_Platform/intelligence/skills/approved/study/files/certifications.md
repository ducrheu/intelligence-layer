# Professional, Licensing, and Standardized Exams

A certification exam differs from a university exam in four ways that change the whole plan: a published blueprint, a scaled score, a booked date you paid for, and study time that competes with a job.

**Before planning any certification**, read `## Courses` (the exam and its date), `## Due` (recertification and simulation cadences), and the shared `~/Clawic/data/bookings/<year>.md` — a booked appointment with a confirmation code lives there, not in the study box.

## Read the Blueprint First

Certification bodies publish the exam's content outline with **percentage weights per domain**. It is the syllabus, the frequency table, and the cut list in one document, and it is free.

- **Allocate study hours by published weight × your gap**, directly. A domain worth 8% of the exam gets 8% of the hours unless you are strong in it, in which case it gets less.
- **The blueprint's verbs matter**: "recognize" and "recall" imply MCQ recognition; "apply", "analyse" and "troubleshoot" imply scenario questions that recognition practice will not prepare you for (SKILL.md Rule 5).
- **Check the blueprint version** against your exam date. Vendors revise them on a schedule, and study material written for the previous version is the standard reason a well-prepared candidate fails.
- Record the domains as topics in `## Topics` with their weights, so coverage state is visible per domain rather than per chapter of some book.

## Scoring Models

Certification scores are usually not percentages, and misreading them wastes weeks.

| Model | What it means | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Scaled score with a fixed pass mark | Raw score converted to a scale to equate difficulty across forms | Your practice-test percentage is not comparable to the scale; only the provider's own scaled feedback is |
| Domain sub-scores | Per-domain performance bands, often only on a fail report | The fail report is the best diagnostic you will get — mine it before rebooking |
| Compensatory scoring | Weakness in one domain can be offset by strength in another | Optimize the total; do not over-invest in a weak low-weight domain |
| Conjunctive scoring | Each domain must be passed independently | The weakest domain sets the outcome; it takes the hours regardless of weight |
| Pass/fail only | No score released | Readiness is judged on question-bank performance alone |

Find out which model applies before building the plan: compensatory and conjunctive scoring produce opposite allocation strategies.

## Adaptive Tests

Computer-adaptive exams (many licensing and admissions tests) select each question from your running estimate.

- **Question difficulty is not feedback.** A test that feels hard throughout is often a test going well; candidates who "felt it was easy" frequently did not pass.
- **Early questions carry disproportionate weight** in some designs, so pacing is front-loaded and skipping is often impossible.
- **You usually cannot return to a previous question.** Two-pass strategies do not apply; commit and move.
- Variable-length adaptive tests may end when the estimate is confident — length is not a signal either.
- Practise on the vendor's own adaptive engine where one exists; a static question bank does not train the pacing.

## Question Banks

The centre of gravity of most certification preparation, and easy to use badly.

- **Use them as retrieval, not as reading.** Answer before revealing, always. A bank clicked through for coverage is the most expensive form of recognition practice available.
- **Read every explanation, including for correct answers.** Correct-for-the-wrong-reason is invisible otherwise, and the explanations are where a good bank's value lives.
- **Track by domain** in `## Topics`, and drill the weakest against the blueprint weights.
- **Do not repeat a bank until you have memorized its items.** Second and third passes on the same bank measure memory of the bank; keep one bank untouched as a final readiness test.
- **Vendor-official practice questions outrank third-party ones** for calibration of style and difficulty, even when there are fewer of them.
- Every miss goes to `errors.md` with its cause, exactly as any other exam (SKILL.md Rule 6).

## Planning Around the Booked Date

- **Book the exam before starting to study**, if the fee and the retake policy allow. A dated commitment is the difference between a certification attempted and one perpetually planned.
- **Backwards plan from the date** with the blueprint as the topic list (`planning.md`). Working students should plan on weekly rather than daily hours.
- **Reserve the last 20% of the timeline** (`planning.md`) **for full-length simulations** — the endurance of a 3-4 hour exam is its own trained capacity, and it is why prepared candidates fail at hour three.
- Employer-funded study frequently comes with a deadline attached to the money. That deadline is a hurdle, not a preference.
- Certification study runs long, so the maintenance ladder matters: material learned in month one needs its spaced reviews or month five is a relearn (`spacing.md`).

## Readiness Signals

Book or confirm the date when these hold, and not on feeling:

- **Vendor-official practice tests consistently above the recommended threshold**, on material not seen before.
- **No domain far below the others** in sub-scores — a conjunctive exam makes this decisive.
- **Full-length timed simulation completed** at the real length, with time to spare.
- **The error log's causes have shifted** from `never encoded` to `misread` and `procedure slip` — the signature of a candidate who knows the material and is now losing performance marks.
- Two consecutive weeks with no new `never encoded` misses in a domain marks that domain as covered.

## Booking, Fees, and Logistics

- **Confirmation code, provider, centre, date, time, cost with its currency and the ID requirements** go to the shared `~/Clawic/data/bookings/<year>.md` — one row, identified by the locator, updated in place if it changes (`memory-template.md`).
- **The password used to book is never written anywhere** under the data folder; the confirmation code itself is not a secret and stays.
- **Reschedule windows and their fees** are policy with deadlines: note the last free reschedule date in `## Due` the day you book, because it is the decision point if preparation slips.
- Check the **ID rules exactly** — name matching the booking, two documents, no expiry inside the window. Turning up with the wrong ID is a forfeited fee and a common failure.
- Where an employer pays, the receipt and the reimbursement deadline are a real deadline; the exam fee belongs in the user's own finance tracking, not here.

## Test-Centre and Online Proctoring

- **Visit the centre's location beforehand** if travel is involved, and budget arrival with a wide margin — late arrival is usually a forfeit.
- **Online proctoring adds failure modes that have nothing to do with the exam**: room scan, clear desk, no second monitor, webcam and bandwidth checks, no interruptions. Run the vendor's system check on the same machine and network days in advance, not on the morning.
- Know what scratch material is permitted — a physical whiteboard, an on-screen notepad, or nothing. Practise with exactly that.
- Breaks are usually scheduled and clock-consuming; know the rules before the day and plan the paper around them.

## Failing and Retaking

- **The fail report is the most valuable document in the process.** Domain bands say exactly where the gap was; plan the retake from them rather than restudying everything.
- **Check the retake policy immediately**: waiting periods (commonly weeks), attempt limits per year, and whether the fee changes. These constrain the retake plan before anything else.
- **Rebook quickly but not blindly**: the material is still warm, which argues for soon, but a retake booked before the diagnosis is a second fee paid for the same gap.
- The most common cause of a second failure is repeating the first preparation method with more hours. Change the method: if the causes were `out of time`, the retake plan is timed drills, not more content (`errors.md`).
- Write the post-mortem as an artifact before rebooking (`memory-template.md`).

## Recertification

- **Renewal cycles are years long and forgotten by design.** The renewal date, the continuing-education requirement and its unit count go into `## Due` the day the certification is earned.
- Continuing-education credits accumulate slowly and are usually cheaper spread across the cycle than bought in the final quarter.
- Where recertification means retaking the exam, the maintenance ladder from `spacing.md` at quarterly gaps costs a fraction of a fresh preparation.
- Certifications that lapse frequently require the full exam again — a lapse is expensive, and it is prevented by one `## Due` row.

**When a certification is planned, booked, sat or renewed**, write the exam and its blueprint domains to `## Courses` and `## Topics`, the booking to the shared `bookings/<year>.md`, the result to `## Results`, and the renewal and reschedule deadlines to `## Due`. A fail report, a blueprint-to-hours allocation that worked, or a post-mortem goes to `artifacts/<kebab-name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line (`memory-template.md`).
